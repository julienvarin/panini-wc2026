2. Likely Questions & Answer Frameworks

A. Motivation & Self-Awareness

"Why this role? Why move from your current domain?"
  • Be honest about wanting the step into people leadership
  • Show you understand the trade: less IC depth, more breadth + people responsibility
  • Connect to S&P specifically — it's central, high-visibility, strategic. Say what excites you
  • Acknowledge what you don't know about the domain. Fresh eyes = strength, less context = gap. Name both
"Biggest difference between senior AE and leading a team of AEs?"
  • Key insight they want: you understand the shift from personal output to team output
  • Your job becomes: unblocking others, setting direction, shielding from noise, coaching
  • You still do hands-on work (role is ~50/50), but your IC work shifts to the hardest problems that unblock the team, not the most interesting ones
"What's your leadership style?"
  • Don't give a textbook answer. Give a real example from your current role where you influenced without authority
  • Name a specific thing you'd do differently as a formal lead vs. informal one

B. People & Team Management

"How would you onboard? First 30-60-90 days?"
  • Weeks 1-2: Listen. 1:1s with both AEs, the ~7 Decision Scientists, understand pain points, read existing dbt models and dashboards
  • Month 1: Understand reliability gaps (the SLO problem), map the data model landscape, understand stakeholder expectations
  • Month 2-3: Propose H2 OKRs based on what you've learned, start structural changes
"An AE is struggling with prioritization between DecS requests and long-term work. How do you help?"
  • Create a visible intake/backlog so requests aren't just Slack messages
  • Teach the AE to push back with data: "this request delays X by 2 weeks"
  • As lead, you own the stakeholder relationship. Take the heat off the AE
  • Standing agreement: e.g., 60% roadmap / 40% reactive, revisit monthly
"How do you split work between 2 AEs across 3 teams (MaPP/MSI + Competitive Intel)?"
  • Current setup: embedded per team — efficient but inflexible (doc says so)
  • Primary/secondary ownership model: each AE owns one area but can cover the other. You as lead are the bridge
  • Rotate quarterly/half-yearly so AEs build breadth and reduce bus factor
"How do you handle a disagreement between an AE and a Decision Scientist on how to model something?"
  • Default: DecS defines what the metric should capture, AE defines how it's built
  • When genuine disagreement: build both versions, show where they diverge, let the stakeholder decide with data

C. Technical / Craft

"The weekly performance dashboard has reliability issues (SLO <4h downtime/30 days). How do you approach this?"
  • Step 1 — Instrument: monitoring on mart models (dbt test failures, freshness checks, data quality alerts)
  • Step 2 — Identify top 3 failure modes: upstream late? Model complexity? Cross-domain dependencies?
  • Step 3 — Tier: not everything is SLO-critical. Which dashboards/marts truly are?
  • Step 4 — Reduce blast radius: decouple marts, add fallback logic (stale data + warning > broken dashboard)
Connects directly to DSA OKRs 2026, Objective 1 — mention this alignment.
"How do you manage cross-domain dependencies for Looker dashboards?"
  • Contracts: explicit interfaces — which tables/columns you depend on, agreed freshness SLAs
  • Alerts: notify when upstream models change schema or timing
  • Thin staging layer: for the most critical paths, maintain a controlled buffer so upstream changes don't immediately cascade
"How would you think about the AI-assisted category mapping quality?" (Case 2.1 probe)
  • Define quality metrics first: precision/recall vs. a human-validated gold standard sample
  • Build automated checks into the pipeline (not just manual PR review)
  • Framework: measure → automate → improve iteratively
"How would you model SKU-based refurbishers vs. C2C listings?" (Case 2.2 probe)
  • Normalize to comparable units: aggregate C2C listings into "product groups" (like SKUs). Refurbisher SKUs already are product groups
  • Compare on: selection breadth (# product groups), depth (stock/availability), pricing (median per group), category coverage

5. Day-of Tips

  • You're internal — use it. Reference specific things you know about Vinted's data stack, dbt patterns, Looker challenges, the AE guild. An external candidate can't do this
  • Lead with frameworks, follow with specifics. For any "how would you..." question: state your approach in 2-3 steps, then give one concrete example
  • Silence is fine. "Good question, let me think about that" > rushed bad answer
  • Show the shift. The #1 thing Fabian evaluates: can this person jump from IC to lead? Every answer should demonstrate you think in terms of team output, not personal heroics
  • Be concrete about the case. Fabian will likely probe your early thinking. Have a 2-minute sketch of each part ready. Don't go deep
  • Don't oversell. Other internal candidates may have more S&P context. Your edge: fresh perspective + proven AE craft + leadership readiness. Own that combination
